# Adventure-style mode: capability survey

**2026-09-27. Research only.** This surveys the current `melee/` decomp and the PC port; it is not a mode design or a claim that every path has been exercised in the PC build. The direction remains open. No game was run for this survey. References to `melee/` identify source in the separate `pc-port` checkout. Earlier undated mode ideas do not override the code and dated port documentation cited here.

**Estimate key.** S = a small integration or authored data pass using existing machinery; M = a new reusable native interface or contained mode subsystem; L = engine-wide behavior, content pipeline, or networking work. Sizes are relative engineering scope for a *first usable slice*, not calendar estimates. A whole campaign, authored stages, animations, writing, and art are additional work. “Port/Geno/Lua” distinguishes existing PC behavior from APIs that would need adding. `gd.*` is the public scripting API; Geno is chiefly the fighter-extension layer, not a generic game-mode framework ([Geno §§1–2](../melee/docs/geno.md#L5), [scripting API](../docs/scripting.md#L151)).

## 1. Non-fighter enemies

**Melee.** The Adventure enemies are primarily **items with enemy-specific logic**, not extra fighter slots or generic stage objects. `ITEM_UNK_ENEMY` is an item hold type; the item-kind enum names Goomba (`Kuriboh`), ReDead (`Leadead`), Octorok (`Octarock`) and `Ottosea` in the first monster range, then Koopa (`Nokonoko`, `Patapata`), Like Like (`Likelike`) and Polar Bear (`Whitebea`) in the second. The old ReDead and Octorok slots are explicitly marked invalid; use the active kinds. There is no kind literally named Topi in this enum; `Ottosea` is the Ice Climbers-related candidate, but its identity should be checked against the actual assets before calling it Topi in player-facing content ([item kinds](../melee/src/melee/it/forward.h#L187), [second range](../melee/src/melee/it/forward.h#L377), [enemy hold kind](../melee/src/melee/it/forward.h#L99)). Their `ItemLogicTable` rows provide per-kind callbacks for state, damage, pickup, throw, and destruction; Octorok also has a distinct stone projectile, and Koopa/Polar Bear have substantial motion/physics/collision state tables. This is built-in behavior, though much of it is tuned for its original stages ([logic rows](../melee/src/melee/it/it_3F2F.c#L132), [Koopa states](../melee/src/melee/it/kinds/itnokonoko.c#L17), [Polar Bear states](../melee/src/melee/it/kinds/itwhitebea.c#L23)). The shared `grZakoGenerator` reads stage-authored spawn entries, checks blast/camera bounds and delays, then dispatches by kind to enemy constructors and tracks each spawned item. Stages explicitly initialize it, including Mushroom Kingdom, Underground Maze and Icicle Mountain ([generator dispatch](../melee/src/melee/gr/grzakogenerator.c#L147), [stage setup example](../melee/src/melee/gr/grkinokoroute.c#L132), [Ice Mountain setup](../melee/src/melee/gr/gricemt.c#L574)). Pokémon are a different item family (`It_PKind_Start` through `Terminate`) with Poké Ball selection and individual item behaviors; they are temporary summons, not general patrol enemies ([Pokémon enum](../melee/src/melee/it/forward.h#L317), [selection logic](../melee/src/melee/it/it_279C.c#L921)).

**Port/Geno/Lua.** Native item and stage code is in the port. `gd.items()` can inspect live enemies by kind, position and motion state, but public Lua has no item/enemy spawn, despawn, damage, target or AI API ([scripting state/API](../docs/scripting.md#L151), [gameplay writes](../docs/scripting.md#L286)). Geno articles reuse Melee items but are fighter-owned projectiles: they have one state, no hurtbox, limited motion, and homing ignores teams. They are not ready substitutes for hittable ground enemies ([Geno article design](../melee/docs/geno.md#L1879), [limits](../melee/docs/geno.md#L2004)).

**Would build — M** for a reusable native enemy-spawn service and stage-independent registration/preload checks, with deterministic authored spawn definitions and a narrow Lua control surface if desired. Merely placing an enum kind on any stage is **not assured**: the generator expects stage spawn markers and item data; collisions, camera bounds and stage-specific AI assumptions need validation. **L** if the goal is a new, freely portable enemy roster with custom animations, AI and authoring tools. First probe: one existing enemy on one existing route stage, then one ordinary stage; verify its data, collision, KO and cleanup.

## 2. Bosses

**Melee.** Master Hand (`Ft_Kind_MasterH`) and Crazy Hand (`Ft_Kind_CrezyH`) are special fighter kinds with dedicated boss helpers and action states, including coordinated logic that looks up both hands. Giga Bowser (`Ft_Kind_GKoops`) is another fighter kind; Adventure explicitly schedules Bowser, transformation cutscenes, then a Giga Bowser fight ([fighter kinds](../melee/src/melee/ft/forward.h#L129), [boss helpers](../melee/src/melee/ft/ftbosslib.c#L158), [Adventure finale](../melee/src/melee/gm/gmadventure.c#L106)). The other Adventure “bosses” are usually regular fighters under encounter rules: two small DKs, giant DK/Kirby, Samus, Link/Zelda, Metal Mario/Luigi, etc. The adventure state table and its setup callbacks select stages, CPU rosters and scale; the tiny-DK setup changes CPU model scale directly ([encounter list](../melee/src/melee/gm/gmadventure.c#L44), [tiny DK setup](../melee/src/melee/gm/gmadventure.c#L1478)). Boss-specific character IDs and special CPU setup are handled in the one-player match builder, rather than by a universal boss component ([one-player builder](../melee/src/melee/gm/gm_17C0.c#L818)).

**Port/Geno/Lua.** The fighters and one-player machinery are present. The scene grammar seeds VS/Training and can name fighter kinds, CPU levels and teams, while one-player modes currently take only `mode=` rather than a full encounter specification ([scene grammar](../_research/scene-launch.md#L131), [1P limitation](../_research/scene-launch.md#L270)). Geno extends a *fighter's* actions, callbacks and articles, so it can help make a bespoke fighter boss, but it does not provide a boss encounter director or health-phase UI ([Geno states](../melee/docs/geno.md#L1429), [articles](../melee/docs/geno.md#L1872)). Lua can read fighters and set percent/stocks, but cannot create or reconfigure a boss mid-scene ([player state](../docs/scripting.md#L155), [gameplay API](../docs/scripting.md#L286)).

**Would build — M for the first reusable slice.** An existing-fighter encounter in a seeded match may be S if its load and camera rules cooperate. A reusable encounter director needs phase triggers, arena lock, health/KO rules, intro/outro and rewards. A new hand-scale bespoke boss or substantial Geno fighter/content work is L. Test the special Hand IDs separately; a legal fighter kind does not establish a safe arbitrary VS encounter.

## 3. Multi-fighter scenes and a party

**Melee.** Match setup already supports multiple human/CPU slots, teams, team-aware outcomes and stock transfer/sharing behavior. The engine stores a team and CPU level per player and applies these in VS setup ([player team/CPU](../melee/src/melee/pl/player.c#L758), [VS setup](../melee/src/melee/gm/gmvs.c#L1628), [team outcome](../melee/src/melee/gm/gmvs.c#L795)). One-player Adventure uses sequential team encounters and persistent stock data for *one selected protagonist*; it is not Brawl's roster of reserve fighters that can be switched in and out ([Adventure team encounters](../melee/src/melee/gm/gmadventure.c#L68), [stock carry and continue](../melee/src/melee/gm/gmadventure.c#L1490), [initial stock](../melee/src/melee/gm/gmadventure.c#L1727)).

**Port/Geno/Lua.** `MELEE_SCENE`/`gd.scene_launch` can already seed up to four VS ports with human/CPU and team assignments; `gd.players()` reports fighter slots 1–6 and CPU flags; `gd.set_stocks` and `gd.set_percent` write the live match ([scene example](../_research/scene-launch.md#L173), [Lua player table](../docs/scripting.md#L175), [Lua writes](../docs/scripting.md#L286)). LAB demonstrates a custom mode using VS machinery with arbitrary humans and CPUs, but its endless time match has no stocks or results ([Geno LAB rules](../melee/docs/geno.md#L457)).

**Would build — M** for friendly-fire rules, ally targeting/follow AI, shared camera/leash and encounter completion across multiple simultaneous fighters. **L** for a persistent party roster with reserve members, switch-on-KO, per-member damage/resources, reinsertion and cross-room continuity. The first playable slice can use two simultaneous fighters and conventional team/stock rules; party switching is a distinct subsystem.

## 4. Level flow: scrolling, rooms, doors, checkpoints

**Melee.** Adventure is an explicit one-player *scene sequence*: IDs are grouped in eight-ID stage blocks, mixing intro/cutscene and `GS_VS` scenes. Its callbacks inspect match exits and choose subsequent scenes, including conditional branches and game-over/continue paths. Brinstar escape, F-Zero Grand Prix, Icicle Mountain, Mushroom Kingdom and Underground Maze are specialized scrolling/traversal stages, not a reusable level graph ([Adventure states](../melee/src/melee/gm/gmadventure.c#L34), [branch example](../melee/src/melee/gm/gmadventure.c#L1461), [mode state routing](../melee/src/melee/gm/gm_1A3F.c#L113)). Route stages have per-stage callbacks, camera offsets, authored map/spawn markers and contact logic; Mushroom Kingdom and Underground Maze initialize enemy generators and manipulate the camera ([Mushroom route](../melee/src/melee/gr/grkinokoroute.c#L89), [camera movement](../melee/src/melee/gr/grkinokoroute.c#L230), [maze stage callbacks](../melee/src/melee/gr/grshrineroute.c#L161)). The stage system can host door-like interactions, but the cited code does not establish a generic room/door/checkpoint service. One-player match setup already carries timers, outcomes, stocks and encounter rules; for example Adventure checks a completed fight's frame count for the Luigi condition ([timer use](../melee/src/melee/gm/gmadventure.c#L1468)).

**Port/Geno/Lua.** LAB is a precedent for adding a game mode and its state table without replacing Training ([Geno LAB flow](../melee/docs/geno.md#L457), [mode implementation](../melee/pc/geno/geno_lab_mode.c#L158)). `gd.scene()`/`on_scene` can observe transitions; `gd.scene_launch` performs a safe soft-reset scene jump, but its documented 1P seeding is limited and its jump does not itself carry a party, room state or checkpoint ([Lua hooks](../docs/scripting.md#L130), [scene launch](../docs/scripting.md#L293), [scene limits](../_research/scene-launch.md#L270)).

**Would build — M** for an authored room graph, door triggers, checkpoint records and timer policies using existing stages and full-scene transitions. **L** for seamless scrolling between arbitrary rooms, streaming stage geometry, persistent live enemies/items and robust camera/respawn across room boundaries. A first slice should choose explicit stage/scene transitions, then verify return location and state reconstruction.

## 5. Cutscenes and presentation

**Melee.** The movie player streams `.mth` (Melee's THP variant) clips, as used by the opening, How-to and ending scenes ([movie player](../melee/src/melee/lb/lbmthp.c#L93), [ending scene](../melee/src/melee/gm/gmmovieend.c#L106)). Adventure also has **in-engine visual scenes** (`Vi*.dat` and `GS_CUTSCENE_*`) sequenced between fights, including Luigi, Brinstar, Star Fox and Giga Bowser ([scene kinds](../melee/src/melee/gm/forward.h#L107), [Adventure placement](../melee/src/melee/gm/gmadventure.c#L61)). These are authored sequences, not a public timeline/dialogue authoring system.

**Port/Geno/Lua.** THP decoding was ported and later dated verification reports working playback; avoid relying on older notes describing the temporary stub ([THP port record](../docs/DEVLOG.md#L1903), [PC startup path](../melee/src/melee/gm/gmopening.c#L144)). `gd.text` and `gd.kit` already draw text, panels, icons and custom mod textures over scenes; the kit is drawing/UI only. There is no public Lua camera, animation timeline, dialogue advancing, subtitle or movie-play call ([drawing API](../docs/scripting.md#L180), [kit API](../docs/scripting.md#L193)).

**Would build — S for the first slice:** still-image/text interstitials and dialogue panels over a paused/separate scene. A deterministic in-engine timeline with camera/fighter cues and skip/restore behavior is M; a custom movie import/playback pipeline and asset packaging are M/L. A short kit-driven interstitial is the cheapest presentation probe.

## 6. Progression and saving

**Melee.** `GmSaveData` persists character/stage unlock bits, per-fighter records, trophy flags/counts and other game history through `lbcardgame`/`lbcardnew` memory-card tasks ([card manifest](../melee/src/melee/lb/lbcardgame.c#L69), [unlock getters](../melee/src/melee/gm/gmmain_lib.c#L1016), [trophy getters](../melee/src/melee/gm/gmmain_lib.c#L203)). Adventure maintains an in-run state and conditional unlock/retry behavior, but this is not a general campaign save schema ([Adventure load/init](../melee/src/melee/gm/gmadventure.c#L1727), [continue point](../melee/src/melee/gm/gmadventure.c#L1490)).

**Port/Geno/Lua.** The PC card shim forwards read/write to Aurora's memory-card implementation, so Melee's native save path exists ([card shim](../melee/pc/platform/shim_card.c#L3), [read/write](../melee/pc/platform/shim_card.c#L340)). Lua has per-script `gd.data_read`/`gd.data_write`, suitable for a local prototype manifest/progress file. Its savestates are scene-bound, transient and disabled online; they are not campaign saves ([script file sandbox](../docs/scripting.md#L86), [data API](../docs/scripting.md#L298), [savestate limits](../docs/scripting.md#L290)).

**Would build — M for a durable first slice:** a versioned, atomic local campaign file with room/checkpoint, party, unlock and collectible IDs, loaded at scene boundaries. A throwaway single-file prototype is S. Integration into Melee's fixed card layout/UI plus peer synchronization is L. Keep campaign progress separate from volatile rollback/savestate memory; define what is committed after death, room exit and disconnect.

## 7. Items, pickups and collectibles

**Melee.** Standard item kinds already include capsules, eggs, Heart Containers, Maxim Tomatoes, food, Poké Balls and coins; item spawning and pickup/collision machinery exists ([common item kinds](../melee/src/melee/it/forward.h#L135), [item spawn path](../melee/src/melee/it/itspawn.c#L179), [coin constructor](../melee/src/melee/it/kinds/itcoin.c#L378)). Melee has a trophy collection with ownership flags, total, gallery and a Figure Get stage; Brawl-style stickers are not a Melee subsystem ([trophy save fields](../melee/src/melee/gm/gmmain_lib.c#L203), [trophy engine](../melee/src/melee/ty/toy.c#L256), [Figure Get stage](../melee/src/melee/gr/grfigureget.c#L80)). The trophy system is tied to its own data/assets and UI; its existence does not make arbitrary new collectibles cheap.

**Port/Geno/Lua.** `gd.items()` exposes live item state, while Geno can add fighter-owned projectile articles. Public Lua has no general item spawn/pickup callback or trophy grant API. The kit can render a collection counter/menu; a script can keep prototype collectible IDs in its data folder ([Lua item view](../docs/scripting.md#L172), [Lua hooks](../docs/scripting.md#L130), [Geno articles](../melee/docs/geno.md#L1879)).

**Would build — M for the first slice:** a small custom pickup type, native touch event and persistent ID set, with kit UI. Generalized authored collectible placement and pickup effects across rooms are M/L. Extending Melee's native trophy gallery/content tables is L; a separate campaign collection is the simpler first path.

## 8. Two-player co-op and netplay

**Melee.** Local multiple fighter slots, teams and CPU allies are proven match concepts; Adventure itself is structured around one selected player and its own stock/outcome assumptions ([player slots/teams](../melee/src/melee/gm/gmvs.c#L1628), [Adventure stock check](../melee/src/melee/gm/gmadventure.c#L1704)). Local co-op still needs shared camera, tether/respawn, pause ownership, doors requiring one/both players, and party/continue rules.

**Port/Geno/Lua.** Current online flow agrees a two-player **VS scene** and rollback begins for `GS_VS` matches. The session closes when that match scene ends; this is not an Adventure-mode network session ([netplay flow](../melee/pc/platform/gw_netplay.c#L8), [agreed VS scene](../melee/pc/platform/gw_netplay.c#L178), [rollback scene gate](../melee/pc/platform/gw_rollback.c#L287)). Gameplay scripts must match by hash and be declared `rollback_safe`; no Lua hook runs on resimulation, Lua memory is not snapshotted, and savestates/pause are unavailable online ([scripting rollback contract](../docs/scripting.md#L108)). Native stage/enemy/party/progression state would need deterministic snapshot coverage, matching content identities and transition/host-authority rules. Geno's article homing currently ignores team affiliation ([Geno limits](../melee/docs/geno.md#L2004)).

**Would build — M for local co-op** on one scene with existing VS team setup. **L for online co-op:** extend netplay session lifetime across rooms, snapshot all new gameplay state, resolve cutscenes/checkpoints/reconnects, and verify rollback determinism. Keep online co-op out of the first vertical slice until the local loop is stable.

## Five highest-leverage capabilities to build first

1. **A custom mode with an explicit room/encounter graph (M).** LAB proves the state-table approach; this gives every later system a place to enter, exit and restore cleanly.
2. **Reusable enemy registration/spawning plus a placed-encounter format (M).** This unlocks side-scrolling play with Melee's existing enemy AI and tests the largest “works on any stage?” uncertainty early.
3. **A two-fighter local team encounter director (M).** It proves ally/enemy targeting, shared camera, defeat and stock rules before implementing reserve-party switching.
4. **Versioned checkpoint/progress storage (S/M).** It lets rooms, party unlocks and pickups accumulate without depending on same-scene savestates.
5. **Kit-based interstitial/dialogue and objective HUD (S).** It can tell a coherent story and show progress while movie and in-engine cutscene authoring remain optional.

This order is about reducing architectural uncertainty, not choosing the final campaign format. An existing-fighter boss can slot into capability 1 or 3 without a general boss framework.

## Ten-line summary

1. Melee has Adventure enemies as item kinds with real behavior; stage code supplies their spawn context.
2. The listed Goomba, Koopa, ReDead, Like Like, Octorok and Polar Bear paths exist; Topi needs asset/name confirmation.
3. Pokémon are temporary item summons, not ordinary roaming enemies.
4. Master/Crazy Hand and Giga Bowser are fighter bosses; most other Adventure bosses are special fighter encounters.
5. Teams and CPU fighters exist, but a persistent, switchable Brawl-style party does not.
6. Adventure sequencing and scrolling stages exist as bespoke 1P code; generic rooms, doors and checkpoints do not.
7. THP movies and in-engine visual scenes exist; the port has movie decode and Lua/kit presentation tools.
8. Melee card saves and trophies exist; a separate versioned campaign save is the smaller initial path.
9. Local co-op can build on VS teams; current online rollback is scoped to one VS match scene.
10. Prototype a custom mode, enemy placement and local team encounter before committing to campaign scale or netplay.
