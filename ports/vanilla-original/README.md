# Vanilla Original: the Courier (original art for Geno slice 4)

An original fighter model, skeleton, 179 animation clips, hurtboxes and ECB for the Geno fighter engine's
no-donor fixture ("Vanilla Original", plan section "Slice 4"). Everything is generated by the scripts in
`src/` from Blender primitives and keyframes: no outside mesh, texture, motion capture, add-on or game data
(credit: Blender and glTF 2.0, `CREDITS.md`). Art and source files only: no engine, game or mod file is touched.

Regenerate everything, one command (headless Blender 5.x, Python 3 with numpy; Pillow only for sheets):

    ports/vanilla-original/build_all.sh            # model, rig, clips, glb, manifest, validation (about 25 s)
    ports/vanilla-original/build_all.sh --renders roundN    # plus review renders into _build/audit-20261003/geno-art/renders/roundN

`BLENDER=/path/to/blender.exe` overrides the default install path. Every Blender job's Windows PID is logged in
`out/blender_pids.txt`; stop one only by that number (never by image name).

## The design

The Courier is a stocky, big-headed runner-messenger built for a 2001-era fighting game seen small. A cream domed
helmet carries a dark visor with two glowing slit eyes (a painted face panel), headphone-like ear discs and a
swept-back crest fin in the team accent colour; round pauldrons sit on a teal tunic with a diamond chest plate,
a buckled belt and a hip satchel; oversized cream gauntlet fists (so punches and grabs read), dark trousers into
big cuffed boots with flat soles, and a four-bone scarf trailing behind that adds secondary motion in every clip.
The silhouette is the crest, the ear discs, the shoulders, the fists and the boots; front and back differ (visor
and diamond plate against scarf and satchel). It is not derived from or modelled on any existing character,
franchise shape, emblem or colour scheme. Four colourways share one mesh and one UV layout and differ only in the
128x64 texture: default (teal tunic, amber scarf), red, blue and green (the tunic and the crest/scarf carry the team
colour; the eye glow follows it).

## Conventions (and why)

* **Units**: 1 unit = 1 Melee unit; the glTF numbers are Melee units, not metres. The fighter is about 11.7 units to the
  helmet top (12.4 to the crest tip), soles at y=0. If the engine lane wants a smaller fighter apply one uniform
  import scale; no clip ever scales a bone.
* **Axes**: authored Z-up, facing -Y, character's left = +X; glTF export is Y-up, facing +Z, left = +X, the same
  handedness and facing as HSD model space. Right-side bones are `*_R`, left `*_L`.
* **Rest pose**: A-pose, arms 38 degrees from vertical, legs straight, feet flat. A-pose because the shoulder is a
  low-poly joint and every attack lifts the arm forward or up; it keeps rest weights away from the armpit crease.
* **Time**: clip frame i is i/60 s, one key per frame on every animated bone, linear interpolation, `frames` = key
  count. Quaternions only. Scale channels exist only because the exporter bakes all channels; they are constant 1.
* **Root motion**: only on bone `trans`, only in the clips flagged `root_motion` in the manifest: EscapeF/B (16
  units), DownForward/Back U/D (14), PassiveStandF/B (14), CliffClimbSlow/Quick (+4.5 forward, +9 up),
  CliffAttackSlow/Quick (+6, +9), CliffEscapeSlow/Quick (+14, +9). Every other clip is in place (the engine moves
  the fighter). Ledge offsets are estimates, not fitted to a stage ledge.
* **Timing contract with the Striker**: the hit pose is on the script's first hitbox frame (`hit_frames`), clip
  length >= the script's iasa/last wait (checked against `vanilla-striker/moves/*.genoasm` by `validate.py`).
  The clips are authored at rate 1.0: set the overlays' `ANIM_RATE` to 1.0 (1.2-1.45 was tuned for Mario's clips).
  Turn clips are continuous in absolute orientation only if the engine flips facing between the declared frames
  (Turn: 4/5, TurnRun: 5/6).
* **Walk/run**: the stance foot moves at constant speed, so a clip is slide-free at `ref.ground_ref_speed` units per
  frame (WalkSlow 0.096, WalkMiddle 0.172, WalkFast 0.301, Run 0.954). Gameplay speeds (Striker walk 1.5, dash 2.0)
  are 2 to 15 times higher: the engine either scales the clip rate or accepts sliding (retail does the same).

## Hurtboxes and ECB (`hurtboxes.json`)

Fifteen capsules: head, chest, torso (on hips), and per side upper arm, lower arm, hand, thigh, shin, foot. Fields
match `gd.hurtboxes`: id, bone, height (high/mid/low), grabbable, endpoints a/b, radius. Endpoints are rest-pose
positions in model space (authoring and glTF); bone-local offsets are the inverse-bind transform of those points.
ECB: diamond {top, bottom, left, right} relative to `trans` with standing (top 11.7), crouch (6.4) and aerial
values, plus a proposed ledge-grab box. The numbers are proposals sized to this body; the engine lane owns the real
ECB sampling policy.

## What the converter must do (engine lane)

1. Read `out/courier.glb` (regenerable) and `manifest.json`. Joints are the glTF skin in `skin.joints` order; roles
   come from `manifest.json` (also in the armature node extras `geno`).
2. glTF Y-up/+Z-forward matches HSD model space, so no axis change is needed; apply an optional uniform scale.
3. Build the HSD skeleton from the bone table (hierarchy, rest matrices), bake the skin (one primitive, at most 4
   influences, so `--pc-palette 64` packs it into one piece), and map costume textures `out/tex/costume_*.png` to the
   four costume slots (same UVs; red/blue/green are the team colourways).
4. Convert clips to HSD joint animations: per clip `frames` keys on `trans`/`hips` (translation) and all deform bones
   (rotation). Drop constant scale channels; keep `trans` translation only where `root_motion` is true.
5. Bind rows to clips with `manifest.json` `motion_rows` (351 rows: own clip, alias, placeholder or no-animation).
6. Turn hurtboxes and sockets into the engine's tables via inverse-bind offsets; use ECB as given or retune it.

## Validation

`validate.py` (plus `validate_blend.py` and `reimport_check.py`, run by `build_all.sh`) checks: at most 4 influences,
normalised weights, no unskinned vertices, budgets (2580 triangles, 29 deform bones, one material), every clip keyed on
every role bone with the declared frame count, no scale/euler channels, loops wrap without a pop, the striking pose
peaks on the hit frame, stance-foot speed equals the declared reference speed, all 351 rows resolved, every Striker
move and required common state has its own clip, hit frames equal the script frames, and a glTF re-import matches
triangles, bones, clips and frame counts. The latest result is in `validation_report.txt`.

## Known gaps (be honest)

* Procedural poses are stiff. A human animator should redo first: Walk*/Run/Dash, the items set (LightGet, HeavyGet,
  ItemThrow*/Swing*/Shoot, LiftWait/Walk), all victim clips (Capture*, Thrown*), ledge climb/attack/roll (offsets are
  estimates), DamageFly*/DamageFall, PassiveWall/Ceil, FlyReflect/Stop*, Win1-3, AppealSR/SL, Rebirth, Lose.
* 151 engine rows are placeholders that alias another clip (item families, special grabs, mushroom, hammer, scope,
  parasol, sleep/bind): see `manifest.json` `motion_rows[].status`. Attack100Start/Loop/End are a placeholder rapid jab.
* No facial or finger animation beyond fist/open; no twist bones; no eye blink; no cloth physics (the scarf follow-through
  is baked per clip). No lying-pose collision data and no paired grab/victim clips tuned to each other's anchors.
* The ECB and ledge box are proposals. Hurtbox radii were eyeballed against the mesh, not tuned for gameplay.
* Costume art is flat-cell colour plus a painted visor; no per-costume portrait/stock icon (slice 6).
* Not seen in the game: nothing here was loaded by the engine.
