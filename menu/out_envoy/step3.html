The idea

A list of stickers down the left is the whole menu. The party and the run stand on the right as a status board you look at but never move onto.

Reading order

  1. The big sticker, top left: what Start does right now.
  2. The list under it: one row per thing you can change, each with its current value on the row.
  3. The party, top right: who is in, two fighters each.
  4. The run, under the party: where the save is, stocks left, keystone, and the rules it was started with.
  5. The white strip: one line about the hovered row.

Buttons

Up / DownMove the one cursor. Player 1 owns it.
AChoose the row, or flip Team attack.
Left / RightChange Run length and NG+ level on their rows.
StartContinue run (or Start run) from any row.
BMain menu.
P2 StartJoin. Hold B on controller 2 to leave.

One column, one cursor, nothing to learn. Player 2 has nothing to do here except inside Fighters, and the party is decoration rather than something you touch.

Main state: two players, a saved run at act 2, fight 3.

The idea

There is no list. The run ticket, the four fighters and the rules are the stickers, laid out as the camp itself, and each player walks their own cursor across them.

Reading order

  1. The ticket, top: the run. Its tabs are the other tickets you could take instead (a new run, a short run).
  2. The party, middle: P1's pair by the left tent, P2's by the right. A on a fighter opens that slot.
  3. The rules, bottom: Team attack, NG+ level, and the Collection, with Back in the corner.
  4. The white strip: one line about what P1 is on.

Buttons

StickMove your cursor between stickers: three rows, at most four across.
AChoose: use the ticket, open a fighter slot, flip Team attack.
L / RFlip to the other tickets from anywhere.
StartUse the ticket on top from anywhere.
BMain menu (P1). P2: hold B to leave.
P2Has a cursor too: own fighters and the Collection. Tickets and rules are P1's.

Both players have something to do, and the party is the thing you touch. It asks for a two-way cursor, and the three rows have to stay this tidy for it to read.

16:9, 853 by 480. Stickers keep their 4:3 positions and size, centred as one block and never stretched. Only the backdrop grows: more hill and sky at each side. Nothing is placed in the extra width, so a 4:3 player misses nothing.

Recommended

C2, the party is the menu. The camp is where two people get ready together, and C2 is the only layout where both of them hold a cursor and the fighters are the things you touch; its three rows (run, party, rules) keep the loose stickers in a fixed reading order. C1 is the fallback if a two-way cursor feels like too much: it is the plainest, but it leaves Player 2 watching.

Original vector art. Fonts: Bricolage Grotesque, Source Sans 3 (SIL OFL). Fighters are neutral silhouettes with placeholder names; drive and keystone names are the step 1 samples; unlock conditions and collection counts are invented for the mock. The grey main-menu frame is the step 2 placeholder for retail parts.
