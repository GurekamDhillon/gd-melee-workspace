Drives, rewards and keystones

Step 4 of the UI design. Three choices: the drive card (D1 or D2), the pick-of-three screen (R1 or R2) and the keystone pick (K1 or K2). The fight HUD, the map, the shop and results are not designed yet.

Outlined in grape ink: a sticker, as before. Sunny: what a cursor is on. Coral arrow is P1, lagoon is P2. A coral burst: something just happened or is about to (level up, timer, set).

The drive card

The same facts on both: family, level, name, rarity, what charges it, what it does, what the next level adds, and a slot when it is going somewhere. Charge is always written as damage dealt. Shown on the violet the game draws behind them.

The idea

A trading card you would stick in an album: a colour band with the family glyph, a ribbon of stars, then the words. Three of them side by side is the picture most players expect from a pick of three.

What it costs

At 126 game pixels wide the sentence wraps to three or four short lines, and the card is a different shape from the thing that ends up in the row and orbiting the fighter, so the chip has to be learned separately.

The most collectable. The hardest to read quickly on a 480-line screen.

The idea

The drive is a round badge, the same object that orbits the fighter, sits in the row and will carry the charge ring. The words ride on a paper tag tied to it, so the sentence gets a full line instead of a narrow column.

One shape from pick to row to fight, and the easiest to read. Less of a "card": three of them make a list, not a hand.

The pick of three

After a fight each player is offered three drives and takes one or skips, both at once, with 20 seconds on the clock. R1 is drawn with the D1 card and R2 with the D2 badge, because each layout only works with that shape. Same steps in both: choose an offer, A, choose which of your two fighters wears it, A.
